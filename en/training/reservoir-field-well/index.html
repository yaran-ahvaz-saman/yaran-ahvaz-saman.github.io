Reservoir rock, reservoir, field and well

Four scales that must not be collapsed into one

From a rock sample to a reservoir volume, a field and an engineered well, each term answers a different question.

After this lesson, you can

  • Distinguish porosity from permeability.
  • Avoid treating reservoir, accumulation and field as synonyms.
  • Explain why one well provides only a limited view of a field.

The real question

Is every porous rock a reservoir, and can one well represent an entire field?

Reservoir rock is material; a reservoir is an evaluated rock-and-fluid volume; a field is a development entity; a well is an access and measurement path.

Working model

01

Porosity: how much space?

Porosity is pore volume divided by total rock volume. Pores can store fluid, but pore volume alone does not describe connectivity or flow capacity.

02

Permeability: how connected are the pathways?

Permeability describes the rock's ability to transmit fluid. Rock may be porous yet poorly permeable, so storage and deliverability are different questions.

03

From rock to reservoir

A reservoir is a defined rock volume with geometry, petrophysical properties, fluids, pressure and evaluable boundaries—not merely a rock name.

04

Field and well

A field may contain several reservoirs and many wells. Each well samples a limited trajectory and is interpreted with seismic, logs, cores and tests.

Bridge to well operations

Where does this sit on the route to wellhead work?

Changes in rock properties and pressure along the well path affect stability, drilling window and casing design. A formation name cannot replace well data.

Glossary

Terms that must remain precise

Porosity
The fraction of total rock volume occupied by pore space.
Permeability
The ability of rock to transmit fluid through connected pores.
Field
A development and production area that may include several reservoirs and wells.

Learning check

Reason through the answer

A sample has high porosity but poorly connected pores. Which property is weak, and what follows?

Answer and reasoning

Permeability is weak. The rock may store fluid, but flow toward a well will be limited without effective connected pathways.
