What is a well and why is it drilled?

From subsurface objective to engineered access and operating life

A well is more than a hole: it is an engineered system for access, data, barriers, completion, production, injection or monitoring.

After this lesson, you can

  • Distinguish a well, a wellbore and a completion.
  • Recognise exploration, development, injection and monitoring objectives.
  • Connect well trajectory to casing and wellhead architecture.

The real question

Why can two wells with similar geological targets require very different trajectories and architectures?

A subsurface target is not the whole design; it is an input. The path must reconcile surface location, pressure, stability, barriers, completion and future operations.

Working model

01

Well objective

An exploration well reduces uncertainty, a development well accesses a known volume, and injection or monitoring wells serve different reservoir-management or assurance functions.

02

Trajectory and depth

Surface location, target position, faults, drilling constraints and access can produce vertical, deviated or horizontal paths. Measured depth is not true vertical depth.

03

Wellbore, casing and barriers

The wellbore is the drilled hole and trajectory. Casing and cement isolate formations and stabilise the path; wellhead equipment manages load and pressure at surface.

04

Completion and well life

After drilling, the well is completed for its purpose. Production, injection, intervention, monitoring, suspension and safe abandonment belong to the full life-cycle view.

Glossary

Terms that must remain precise

Wellbore
The physical hole and trajectory drilled through rock.
Measured depth (MD)
Distance along the well path from the measurement datum.
True vertical depth (TVD)
Vertical distance from the surface datum to a subsurface point.

Learning check

Reason through the answer

A deviated well reaches its target from one surface location, and MD exceeds TVD. What does each quantity measure?

Answer and reasoning

MD is distance measured along the well path; TVD is the vertical distance between the surface datum and the subsurface point.
