Reading a first well schematic

Hole size, casing OD, cement, annulus, shoe and wellhead datum

A schematic is not a scale drawing; it is a compressed language for boundaries, strings, depths, barriers and interfaces.

After this lesson, you can

  • Identify hole, casing and cement boundaries.
  • Read shoe depth separately from top of cement.
  • Avoid treating a conceptual schematic as an installation drawing.

The real question

How can a simple schematic distinguish hole diameter from casing outside diameter?

Read a schematic outside-in and top-down: formation, hole, cement, casing, internal annulus, shoe and surface interface.

Working model

01

Title, revision and datum

Before geometry, verify well identity, date, revision, units and depth datum. A diagram without them is insufficient for execution.

02

Hole diameter and casing OD

Hole size is the drilled-section diameter; casing OD is pipe outside diameter. Their gap forms the cementing annulus, and the assembly must pass through the previous string ID.

03

Shoe, cement and annulus

The shoe marks the string end. Cement top may be at surface or lower. 'Annulus' changes meaning according to the two adjacent boundaries.

04

Surface interface

At the top, string suspension, seals and head/spool components are shown. Installation requires the approved manufacturer drawing and stack-up.

Glossary

Terms that must remain precise

Hole size
The nominal diameter of a drilled section.
Casing OD
The nominal outside diameter of casing.
Top of cement
The evaluated upper extent of cement in the relevant annulus.

Learning check

Reason through the answer

A schematic shows a 17½-in hole and 13⅜-in casing OD. Does their numerical difference directly equal annular thickness?

Answer and reasoning

No. Diameter difference is divided by two for radial clearance, while actual hole size, eccentricity, centralisation and tolerances also affect real geometry.
